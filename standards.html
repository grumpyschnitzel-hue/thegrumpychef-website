What’s on the page

Five blocks. Three lines each. Nothing you don’t need.

Most “values” docs are wallpaper. This one’s a brief sheet. Each block names a behavior, not a noun. Each line is short enough to call out across a hot line.

01

How we treat each other

Private corrections, public praise, zero tolerance for harassment. Three lines that set the floor.

02

How we talk in service

Standard calls. No debates on the line. Confirm allergies and modifications. Communication that prevents accidents.

03

What good looks like

Cold below 5C. Hot above 63C. Stations cleaner than they were found. Three rules for plates. No negotiation.

04

How we hold the line

Coaching, then documented, then role review. Direct line to the chef for safety. Weekly 15-minute huddle.

05

Why people stay

A real ladder per role. Structured training, not sink-or-swim. Consistent performers get the schedule they want. The retention block other sheets pretend doesn’t matter.

Who this is for

Owner-operators and head chefs who are tired of explaining the same thing twice.

If your brigade keeps making the same mistakes, the problem isn’t the people — it’s the missing standard. This sheet is one weekly briefing tool that pre-decides the answer to the questions that come up every shift anyway.

Used in any kitchen running a real service: independent restaurants, hotel kitchens, catering operations, ghost kitchens. Anywhere standards have to hold up under pressure.

You already know where it's broken.

You found the leak. Maybe you ran the calculator. Maybe you bought the Kit. You can see the number now.

But knowing where $15K disappears every year and actually building the system that stops it from coming back are two different things.

Most operators fix the leak once, then watch it reopen six weeks later. Because the fix was a reaction. Not a system.

  • Staff calls in sick and you're scrambling again
  • Food cost spikes and you make reactive menu changes mid-week
  • You push through exhaustion instead of delegating
  • You make exceptions for employees who don't deserve them
  • You skip the daily count because you're too tired
  • You improvise under pressure instead of following a protocol

Every one of these is a system failure. Not a character flaw.

Three weeks. Three phases. One operating system.

Week 1

Interruption

Break the automatic reactions that have been running your restaurant on autopilot. Install "The Pause" between stimulus and response. Add friction to the comfort patterns that are bleeding you dry. You can't change what you can't see.

Week 2

Criteria-Based Systems

Replace "what do I feel like doing?" with pre-decided responses for every recurring situation in your operation. Staff no-show? There's a protocol. Food cost spike? There's a protocol. Cash flow crunch? Protocol. No emotion. No negotiation. Execute.

Week 3

Pressure Testing

Deliberately add stress to prove the system holds when you need it most. Close every "just this once" exception loop. If the system survives Week 3, it's real. If it breaks, you know exactly where — and that's your next fix.

Where this came from.

In April 2022, I lost everything. Restaurant gone. $370K in debt. Drunk. Broken.

Everyone said "work harder." I was already working 100-hour weeks. That wasn't the problem. I was the problem. No systems. Just chaos and hustle and hoping tomorrow would be different.

Recovery taught me something the restaurant industry won't tell you: you can't motivation your way through addiction. And you can't hustle your way to profitability.

You need systems that hold when you're broken.
